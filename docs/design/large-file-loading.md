# Large canvas loading

Large pages preserve every top-level screen box and use a layer budget for full editable DOM instead of a four-screen ceiling. The automatic budget is 1,800 expanded layers on devices reporting at most 4GB RAM (also the conservative fallback), or 3,600 on larger devices. Nearby screens begin preparing at a minimum displayed side of 64px, with 48px retention hysteresis, and compete by distance and cost. A 900px margin prepares approaching screens. Overview screens use real cached raster images. Direct selection, text/path editing, comments and targeted navigation can temporarily mount the necessary screen beyond this automatic budget.

## Real screen previews

The open page requests its initial paginated catalog immediately, 200 screens per batch. Edits refresh it after a short debounce. Already-open large designs also prepare previews in the background after edits, without waiting for zoom or navigation. This is bounded to watched pages (or the first page), at most 16 files and eight watched pages per file; it never reopens closed files. Bogen derives fingerprints from expanded screen HTML, component instances, conditional CSS, branch, variables and shader parameters. One background renderer captures each changed screen at a time, yielding to foreground rendering and pausing under the existing memory-pressure guard. Up to four sequential captures reuse one page in a short-lived isolated browser context, which is then closed completely. Memory pressure and foreground work are checked between screens. The small raster is downsampled from the completed capture without a second viewport/layout/paint pass. The browser is released when work drains or memory is low.

Two WebP sizes (up to 768px and 128px on the longest side) keep overview image decoding small. The private, device-local `screen-previews` directory holds only derived images and measured geometry, capped at 128MiB or 1,024 records with oldest-generation eviction. Cache files are owner-readable; access is restricted to the owner or paired phone, not peers. Closing or deleting a file cancels its queued work; deletion also removes its cached images. A capture racing with an edit is discarded. Rendering never modifies the document.

Existing preview images stay visible during pan, zoom, recapture and the transition to editable DOM. New images decode before replacing the previous pixels; the editable DOM waits briefly for visible images and fonts before uncovering. Images live outside the design DOM and follow the same camera transform. A stale image is retained only within its original file, page and branch. Higher-resolution previews have a decoded-pixel budget of 1.5 million on devices reporting at most 4GB (the fallback), or 3 million on larger devices; other screens retain their small image.

First-time uncached previews still fill in asynchronously. A screen without an image retains its box and a Loading hint; opening it loads its real content. Unchanged previews survive service restarts. This cache is not synced across devices. Dynamic content changing outside the document does not automatically invalidate a snapshot; previews are still images, not live embeds.

Fixed clipped screens retain exact dimensions. Absolute clipped hug screens use fresh cached geometry when available, otherwise the last local measurement or an explicitly approximate initial estimate. Document styles and exported designs never use proxy measurements. Transformed, overflowing, relative/content-width or rule-dependent screens retain the full rendering path rather than guessing layout. Full Preview and export use their existing complete-design renderers. Explicit bulk edits can exceed the automatic layer budget.

## Startup and qualification

Model discovery is independent of document startup. The editor reads loaded settings without launching Claude/Codex model catalogs. Explicit model pickers still probe their CLI.

Qualification distinguishes first paint, first editable content, cold raster generation, warm overview, mounted layers, focus, pan, visible MCP edits, heap and total process/cgroup memory. A generated fixture and one machine are not universal stability guarantees. Tests use isolated data; ARM64 tests enforce memory/CPU/time bounds and host reserve. Failed or resource-limited attempts remain failures in the evidence.
